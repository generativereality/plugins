# The brief

Write the brief before anything is made, and keep it short: one screen. A strategy or growth skill
that sends work here may already have written most of it; keep its fields and add what is missing.

## Template

```markdown
---
piece: <slug>
purpose: brand | audience | teaching | product | personal
status: open | working | picking | making | done
created: <date>
budget: <money cap> · <time>
---
# Brief: <what is being made, in plain words>

**Next step it should cause:** <follow, reply, try it, sign up, share with one person, understand X>
**Measured by:** <the link or tag, the count to read, and when to read it>
**Reader:** <who, in their situation> · **meets it:** <where, on what device> · **awareness:** <stage>
**The one idea:** <one sentence>
**Channel and format:** <place, form, length/ratio, its rules and account requirements, with links>
**Material:** <the person's own words (source), real screenshots/photos/recordings, real numbers (source)>
**Voice:** <whose; where their voice notes and lessons are>
**Sent back before:** <earlier versions and the reasons, in the person's words>
**Make:** <how many hooks, angles, formats; which media>
```

## Purpose decides what good looks like

| Purpose | Good means | The usual mistake |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand** (what people think of a person, company or product) | the same few things said, shown and repeated until boring; recognisable without the logo | a different style every time, or slogans with nothing behind them |
| **Audience** (people who come back) | a series or a format people can expect; something they save, send or reply to | one-off posts; chasing reach over the people already there |
| **Teaching** (someone understands or can do something) | the reader can do it after one read or watch; the picture carries the hard part | covering everything; abstraction without a worked example |
| **Product** (someone tries, buys or understands a product) | the real product doing something surprising, then one clear next step | describing the product; internal numbers presented as the story |
| **Personal** (family, friends, a memory) | one relationship, one small moment, a line that lands; it feels like them | polish that reads as an ad; a slideshow with a stock voice |

## Awareness: where the reader already is

Decide this before the first line (Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*):

| The reader | Open with |
|---|---|
| Most aware: knows it, wants it | the thing itself and the offer |
| Product aware: knows it, unconvinced | a new proof, or what changed |
| Solution aware: knows the result they want | that result, then how |
| Problem aware: feels the problem, knows no fix | the problem in their words, then the way out |
| Unaware | a story or situation they recognise; never the product |

A stranger in a feed, a forum or a news site is problem aware at best. Someone browsing a directory
of tools is solution aware. Only people who already know the person or product are product aware.

## The next step and its measure

Choose one next step per piece. Write down what counts as success before making anything, with the
number you expect: views at 24 hours as a multiple of the person's normal, clicks, replies, sign-ups
in the week after, or for personal pieces whether it was shared and with whom. Without a prediction
there is nothing to learn from the result (`copy.md` §after).

## When the request is vague

"Make something for LinkedIn about X" is not a brief. Fill in what you can from the project (its
positioning, earlier pieces, the person's notes) and then ask the person **one** question that only
they can answer, about the material, not about the craft. Ask *"what happened the first time you
saw it work?"*, never *"what should the post say?"*.
